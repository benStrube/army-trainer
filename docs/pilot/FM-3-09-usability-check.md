# FM 3-09 deck: usability check (MVP criterion 5)

**Goal:** after a 15-minute walkthrough of `out/decks/FM-3-09.pptx`, a junior Soldier who doesn't know FM 3-09 answers **at least 8 of 10** basic "what do I have to do" questions (`docs/PROJECT_SCOPE.md` §8).

## How to run it
1. **Participants:** 1–3 junior Soldiers (E-1 to E-4) who haven't studied FM 3-09. A fires MOS is fine, but don't use FIST veterans.
2. **Walkthrough:** 15 minutes, led by an instructor. 37 slides in 15 minutes is about 25 seconds a slide, too fast for every slide. Spend the time on the **core path** and click through the rest:
   - **Core path** (deck slide numbers in `out/decks/FM-3-09.pptx` after WP 5.1, 38 slides): 3, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 18, 19, 24, 25–26, 27, 34–35.
   - If the deck is re-rendered and numbers shift, go by the titles in the key below.
3. **Questions:** ask them right after the walkthrough, closed deck, in any order. Accept any answer that matches the key in substance.
4. **Record:**
   - each person's score
   - which questions were missed
   - any slide they said was confusing

   Put the results in the WP 5.1 handover.

## Questions and answer key
| # | Question | Key (slide; source) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is a forward observer's main job? | Locate targets accurately, then call for and adjust fire support (slide 8, "Fire support people at company and platoon"; para 2-19) |
| 2 | When must "danger close" be called for mortars or artillery? | When the rounds are predicted to land within **600 meters** of friendly troops (slide 12, "Danger close: when to call it"; para 3-29) |
| 3 | During a danger close mission, what is the largest adjustment for mortars or artillery? | **100 meters**, using the creeping method (slide 12, "Danger close: when to call it"; para 3-29) |
| 4 | What must happen before a *suspected* target is engaged? | It must be confirmed (slide 10, "Target or suspected target?"; para 2-76) |
| 5 | A fire mission isn't pre-cleared and no permissive measure allows it. What must happen? | It must be positively cleared (slide 15, "Positive clearance of fires: what to get"; para 3-50) |
| 6 | Can fires go into a no-fire area? | No: fires or their effects are prohibited, except (a) the HQ that set it approves them for a mission, or (b) the enemy in it engages friendlies and the engaged commander responds with minimal force (slide 19, "Can fires go into a no-fire area?"; para B-33) |
| 7 | What does a restrictive fire line do? | Fires or their effects are prohibited from crossing it; it sits between friendly forces moving toward each other (slide 18, "Lines on the map and what they mean for fires"; para B-39) |
| 8 | When may you look at the sun through the telescope? | Only with a sun filter. If the filter is damaged or lost, use the card method (slide 27, "Sun observations: protect your eyes"; para C-32) |
| 9 | Your GPS is jammed and you have no anti-jamming gear. What do you do? | Block the signal with your body and pivot 90 degrees every two minutes, or put the receiver in a hole six inches deep. If neither works, move (slide 24, "GPS jammed? Get the signal back"; para C-11) |
| 10 | Name one way to prevent fratricide from the deck. | Any one of: positively verify identity when forces may be intermixed; use and share fire support coordination measures; take special care at night or in limited visibility (slide 14, "Fratricide: stopping friendly-fire casualties"; para 3-44) |

**Pass:** 8 or more correct for each participant. When a question is missed, also look at its slide (for example, a slide too dense to read in the time).
